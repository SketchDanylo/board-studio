# Board Studio for chess.com

A Tampermonkey userscript that restyles chess.com and adds a few training helpers.

**[Install the small loader](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SketchDanylo/board-studio/main/board-studio-loader.user.js)** — with Tampermonkey installed, open that link and press *Install*. Loader 6.0.1 resolves the current GitHub commit, then downloads the full script at that immutable revision on each page load. This avoids stale raw `main` responses. If the revision lookup fails, it tries a known release containing the crown clipping fix; if downloading fails, it uses the cached script. A fallback never replaces a newer cached version. Update older loaders once to receive this fix.

The [full script](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SketchDanylo/board-studio/main/board-studio.user.js) is also available for direct installation; it updates only when its `@version` is bumped. Use either the loader or the full script, not both.

## What it does

Everything is switched on and off from the **Styles** item in chess.com's left sidebar.

- **Custom style** – glass sidebar and menus with flow-in and hover animations.
- **Double square** – right-click a square for your first color, again for your second, a third time to clear.
- **Custom arrows** – soft spring-animated arrows, L-shaped for knights, in your color.
  - **Aim assist** – an arrow drawn from a piece snaps to the nearest square that piece can reach.
- **Arrow Eval** – independently colors native and custom arrows by estimated move grade, with smooth transitions as the engine refines its search. Enable it directly below **Custom arrows**.
- **Opening book** – the book from [Chess Coach](https://sketchdanylo.github.io/chess), with thicker arrows for more popular moves.
- **Winning side** – a crown on the avatar of whoever is ahead, from chess.com's eval bar or, where there is none, a built-in Stockfish that keeps searching deeper. Every position is remembered and the game's earlier positions are looked at in spare time, so stepping back through the moves shows who was winning at that point.
- **Rate Moves** – chess.com Game Review-style badges (Brilliant, Great, Best, Excellent, Good, Book, Inaccuracy, Mistake, Miss, Blunder) on the move that led to the shown position, with the move's squares tinted; works while stepping back through the game. A rating shows at once from a quick look and is replaced as deeper looks come in (depth 8 to 20); a move that gives material away is checked deeper (24) before it is called a mistake or a Miss. Where chess.com's own Game Review has rated a move, its rating stands and Board Studio draws nothing over it.
  - **Rate Self** – also rate your own moves once you have made them (off: only the opponent's).
- **Clutch pacing and win plan** – Clutch spends its clock like a person of its rating: the time left shared over the moves to come, less for book moves, recaptures and forced moves, more for hard ones, every move included (premoves at once only under 15 s); a 1-minute game uses about three quarters of the clock. Playing to win it only uses the full engine while not ahead; ahead, its rating plays (lifted while the lead is small), so an inaccuracy is not always punished at once. Measured against Stockfish skill 4: still 6/6 wins at 800 and 1500, with games of about 110 plies instead of 80.
- **Clutch → Loop Games (6.69)** – games against the computer only: when a game ends, a new one starts after a random 0 to 20 seconds. Plan A is chess.com's own button for the same time control ("New 1 min"), plan B closes the game-over window and uses another new-game button (New Game, Rematch, Play Again, the setup's Play), plan C opens a fresh bot page and starts from its setup. It waits while the Fake Review is open, and never acts in live games.
- **Clutch → Gobble, Witty's lines as bands (6.68)** – Gobble: greedy and quick. Among the moves Clutch weighed, the capture that wins the most material it likes (a piece before a pawn) is taken whenever the decided outcome allows it; it premoves more (also on their clearly best reply, in every mode) and at once with under a minute; it plays about 40% faster, 65% faster in the endgame with no long thinks there. With Witty Alieeeen on too, Witty leads until the endgame (no queens and at most six pieces, or queens and at most three); in the endgame Gobble takes over and Witty is off. Against Stockfish skill 4 at 1500 playing to win: 6/6 with Gobble (41 grabs) as without. Witty's lines (pins, open lines, checks, a slider's planned move) are now marked square by square along the whole file, rank or diagonal, swept in like an edited video, with the target squares solid over them.
- **Pawn endings on premoves (6.67)** – in a pawn ending (kings and pawns only) Clutch works out on the opponent's time a move that keeps the outcome it plays for against every legal reply they have, checked one by one with the engine, and premoves it; it goes out the instant they move, whatever the clock. Move after move that is the premoved plan: winning, the pawn push (or the king step, where the push would let their king catch the pawn) that wins whatever they do; drawing, the move that holds against all of them; losing, the natural push of the race (the premove a player gets wrong) as long as it does not turn the game into a win. Where the right move depends on theirs (the opposition), nothing is premoved.
- **Losing slowly, Witty's highlights, a hand that lifts pieces (6.66)** – playing to lose no longer makes a mistake every move: the bar is meant to go down along a slow schedule (about level by move 8, then a third of a pawn or so a move); at or under it Clutch plays ordinary moves of its rating, above it a move drifts the eval down (likelier the further above, every move when ahead), never in one big blunder unless far behind schedule. Against Stockfish skill 4 at 1500: still 6/6 lost, games 70 -> 111 plies, own ACPL 104 -> 68, moves giving away 3+ pawns 8% -> 4%; at 800 against skill 0: 6/6 lost, 58 -> 69 plies, 27% -> 18%. Witty now marks the board with highlighted squares only (no arrows): the pinned piece and what is behind it, the checked king, targets, the end of a line he holds (orange), where his plan goes (green), the squares around their king he hits. Show Mouse's hand also lifts its pieces Hikaru style: picked up, moved quickly over a square or two it could go to, and put back (drawn only).
- **Human move times (6.65)** – Clutch's think times now spread the way a person's do (chess.com's time bars no longer show the same few seconds every move): mostly short moves, some played at a glance, now and then a long think (more often in a hard position), with the next move or two quick after it because the plan is made; opening moves out of book quicker. The clock is used as before overall. With No Limit and no clock a typical move takes about 2 s and the longest stays at 17 s; in timed games a long think is bounded by the clock instead of 17 s.
- **Witty marks up the board, a fidgeting hand (6.64)** – Witty's markup now looks like his streams: after his move, up to four arrows and three highlighted squares stay while the opponent thinks (about 7 s): the pin (and the pinned piece in red), the check, the piece the move goes after or the weak pawn next to the king it hits (the f7 a knight on g5 eyes), the long open lines his bishops, rooks and queen hold (towards the king first), the next moves of his plan in yellow (a knight's outpost in green, a pawn storm at a castled king), and the squares around their king his pieces already hit, in red. On a long think he shows the move he is looking at and the reply he expects. Show Mouse's hand fidgets like Hikaru while it waits: it clicks its own pieces (a quick tap or two, the piece it is about to move most often), drawn only, nothing is clicked on the real board.
- **Witty's arrows, a steady hand (6.63)** – with Witty Alieeeen on, after a move of a bishop, rook or queen Clutch draws the arrows a streamer would for a few seconds: the pin it makes (through the pinned piece to what is behind), or the long open line it now holds, and the move its plan has next when that puts a bishop or queen on a long diagonal. Show Mouse's hand no longer jumps: it remembers where it was and drifts slowly over the board between moves.
- **Witty at full strength (6.62)** – with Elo 3501 and Witty Alieeeen on, the engine's own move is searched exactly as without the style, then the position is scanned for sacrifices and the best one is searched at full depth against it; a sacrifice that keeps the game crushing (+3, or a mate up to five moves longer) is played. Against Stockfish skill 10: 6 wins in 6, 12 sacrifices and several sacrificial mates, as accurate as plain 3501.
- **Clutch Time: No Limit (6.61)** – the Time slider goes down to 0, shown as a red "No Limit": Clutch then plays the whole game. With a clock it paces itself by that clock (a 15-minute game is played like one); in a game without a clock it plays as if it had five minutes, obvious moves quick, a hard one at most 17 s.
- **Witty and losing, more real (6.60)** – Witty sees a mate of about three moves (a longer one only very rarely), plays ordinary moves quicker and premoves freely under 30 s; losing on purpose goes down mostly through wrong sacrifices (an aggressive move that looks right at the player's depth) instead of hanging pieces: material lost from a level position at 800, 16 times in 6 games before, once after.
- **Clutch, more human (6.55–6.58)** – a recapture in an exchange goes out at once (premoved or not); losing on time drains the clock a little each move (no long sits) and only freezes in the last seconds; losing on the board goes down in small steps (no piece thrown away from a level game), with hand slips three times as common; a slip of the hand never gives material away when winning or drawing; playing for a draw well ahead, a stalemate on the board is played at once, as a slip of the hand next to the intended square.
- **Clutch → Witty Alieeeen** – Clutch plays like Witty_Alien: his gambits (Alien and Martian against the Caro-Kann, Elephant and Englund as Black) where its rating leaves room to win them back, and the sacrifice or the move that tears the king open (or a mate that starts with a sacrifice) instead of the plain best move, only while the outcome Clutch plays for holds. When it spots a sacrifice it thinks it over (a search as deep as its rating looks, about 10 at 800 and 16 at 2200, so a sacrifice the player would not see is not found; then a safety check) and takes longer over the move; once played, it follows the line through for a few moves, in full at a high rating and partly at a lower one (a bishop won where the line wins a rook), playing those moves well above its rating. With it on: 6/6 wins at 800 and at 1600 against Stockfish skill 4. Benchmarked at 1500 against Stockfish skill 6: 7 wins, 1 draw, 0 losses with it on and off; forced into the Alien/Martian knight sacrifices: 7 wins, 3 draws, 0 losses in 10.
- **Fake Review** (test feature) – the Game Review button opens a review made locally with the built-in engine, in chess.com's style: accuracy, the count of every kind of move, game rating, opening/middlegame/endgame, an evaluation graph, the moves with their ratings and a coach line for the move on the board, plus a Best arrow. A thorough first sweep (depth 16) is followed by ever deeper passes for as long as the review is open, the move on the board first. chess.com's own review is not used. The cogwheel in the review's header has **Show best move arrows**: a green arrow for the engine's move wherever the move played was not it. A Brilliant is shown only once it holds at depth 20 (a forced mate needs no more), and a piece that was lost whatever was played (a king forced out of check, say) is never counted as a sacrifice. The analysis of the last four reviewed games is saved in the browser, so reopening one shows it at once and carries on from where it stopped. After the first sweep the review works in rounds: the moves that may be Brilliant get a long look of their own, then every position gets the same time (3 s in the first round, doubled each round). Sacrifices are measured in chess.com's piece values (1/3/3/5/9), so an exchange left hanging counts, as on chess.com.
- **Take squares** – move dots turn red where your piece could be taken.
  - If Chess.com's own move hints are absent or disabled, Board Studio draws legal move dots itself. This also works before an arrow controller or canvas sprite hook is available. Legal destinations appear without waiting for the engine.
  - **Show Blunders** – optional engine mode: move dots and capture rings morph into translucent red crosses for likely blunders, appearing from the first available engine scores and updating as analysis deepens. Crosses span about half a square. Their outlines morph back into dots or capture rings when a move is cleared, with only one marker visible at a time. Hold or keep a piece selected for deeper analysis. Supports normal and canvas boards.
- **Ghosts** – each arrow moves a see-through copy of the piece, so you can plan lines.
  - **Ghost AI** – after a legal drawn move, searches the opponent's replies to **depth 18**, then shows up to four alternative ghost moves. Green is the engine's top reply, yellow second, orange third, and red fourth. These colors indicate rank, not move grades. It works with native and custom arrows and uses the Ghosts opacity slider.

Works on both chess.com board types (the normal one and the Arcade / Natural canvas board).

Ghost AI is off by default; enable **Styles → Ghosts → Ghost AI**. It follows legal drawn continuations, or starts a new variation when you draw another legal move from the original position. It previews the opposing side after your move; it does not play moves on the real board. Promotions default to a queen for drawn moves, while engine replies retain their actual promotion choice. Castling replies include the rook ghost. No replies appear until the complete depth-18 ranking arrives; positions with fewer than four legal replies show fewer alternatives. Clearing arrows, switching off either Ghost AI or Ghosts, changing the position, or hiding the tab cancels the old preview. Mate and other terminal positions have no reply preview.

## Crown behavior

The winning-side crown works on bot and live boards, using the visible evaluation bar when available and the downloaded engine otherwise. An equal score shows no crown. Toggle it with **Styles → Winning side**. Version 5.5.3 supports both the current `player-top` / `player-bottom` avatar containers and the older board layout IDs.

## Board support

Version 5.7.1: Stockfish (Show Blunders, Arrow Eval, the crown without an eval bar) runs against bots, in analysis, puzzles and practice, and on a game page once the game has a result, so finished games can be reviewed. It never runs while a game against a person is being played: that is engine help and breaks chess.com's fair-play rules. Version 5.6.10 morphs dots into crosses and back on DOM, canvas, and fallback boards. Non-blunder capture-risk dots stay red. The opening book retains its existing live-game restriction.

## Arrow Eval estimates

Version 5.7.0 adds **Styles → Arrow Eval**, off by default. Draw an arrow for a legal move by the side to move. Both native and custom arrows receive colors for Brilliant, Best Move, Excellent, Good, Book Move, Inaccuracy, Mistake, Blunder, and Missed Win. Disabling the option restores the original colors. Arrows without a legal move remain unchanged; promotion arrows assume a queen.

Colors transition over 460 ms and respect reduced-motion preferences. All legal moves are evaluated, so the comparison includes alternatives outside the drawn arrows. Results update from the first engine scores; Brilliant additionally requires a complete search iteration of at least depth 12, a sound position, and a legal material sacrifice rather than an equal trade. A conservative check excludes positions where another move is already clearly winning. The bundled opening book identifies Book Move; Missed Win estimates a missed winning continuation that leads to equality or worse. Analysis stops when arrows are cleared, the option is disabled, or the tab is hidden, and restarts for a changed position.

These are **local Stockfish estimates, not exact Chess.com Game Review labels**. Chess.com's rating-dependent expected-points model and special-move rules are not fully public. Brilliant detection may miss sacrifices, and deeper analysis can change any grade. [Chess.com's classification explanation](https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8572705-how-are-moves-classified-what-is-a-blunder-or-brilliant-etc) describes the differences.

## Blunder estimates

Version 5.6.4 also flags a loss of at least 250 centipawns when both moves have at most 0.01 expected points. This handles positions where Stockfish rounds every candidate to a loss: dropping a queen must not disappear merely because the position was already bad. This exception does not apply to positions that remain winning or to shortening an already-forced mate. A regression test reconstructs the reported board and checks that Qe2, Qf3 and Qxg4 receive crosses from the real engine at depth 13.

Version 5.6.3 preserves the engine and its scores when native and fallback hint renderers hand off to each other, and restores overlay layers if the board redraw removes them. No cross is expected when that piece has no moves classified as blunders. Crosses describe destinations before moving, rather than annotating the last played move.

Enable **Styles → Take squares → Show Blunders** (off by default). It keeps capture-risk dots on other squares while morphing blunder destinations into crosses, and evaluates every legal root move so a bad move is compared against the best move in the whole position. Analysis stops when selection is cleared, the board is removed, the tab is hidden, or the option is disabled. Changing positions discards the old worker and its results. In the absence of native hints, legal dots come from the bundled chess.js rules; engine startup or failure never removes those ordinary dots.

These are Stockfish estimates, **not guaranteed Chess.com Game Review classifications**. [Chess.com's published blunder cutoff](https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8572705-how-are-moves-classified-what-is-a-blunder-or-brilliant-etc) is a loss of 0.20 expected points, using a rating-dependent model. This script instead uses Stockfish's win/draw/loss probabilities, with an additional 200-centipawn loss requirement unless forced mate is involved. Scores appear as soon as each candidate and the best move have evaluations at the same depth; there is no minimum-depth or repeated-verdict delay. Existing hints remain until their own fresh evaluation arrives, and are revised immediately if that verdict changes. A promotion destination is marked only after all four promotion choices have been evaluated as blunders. Early estimates can change substantially as the search deepens; engine startup and the first evaluation still take some time.

## Development checks

Run `npm ci`, `npm run check`, and `npm test`. Tests cover the settings, both hint renderers, evaluation revisions, stale results, promotion choices, and the bundled Stockfish executable on mating blunders for both colors.

The opening book and Stockfish are not inside the script (they made it 2.8 MB and Tampermonkey slow). They are the files in `assets/` and are downloaded the first time they are needed: the book when a board shows, Stockfish when an engine feature starts. The loader points the script at `assets/` of the same commit and Cache Storage keeps them, so later page loads do not download them again. Without the loader the script falls back to `main`.

Run `node scripts/bundle-rules.cjs` after changing the chess.js dependency to rebuild the embedded legal-move rules. Tests also cover boards with no native hints, pins, castling, en passant, and engine startup failure.
